## About

I'm a Technical Product Manager currently at **SellMato**, where I own product roadmap, feature definition, and seller-onboarding experience. Before moving into product, I spent time as a DevOps engineer and technical lead, building CI/CD pipelines and cloud infrastructure for production systems.

That engineering background didn't get left behind — it's the reason my product decisions hold up technically. I can write a PRD, scope it with engineering in the same session, and understand exactly where the complexity (and risk) actually lives in the system.

<br/>

**Technical Product Manager** · 2024 – Present

- Own the product roadmap and translate business goals into prioritized features
- Write user stories, acceptance criteria, and business requirements for engineering
- Lead seller-onboarding product work, from experience design to monetization decisions
- Work directly with engineering on feasibility, sequencing, and trade-offs

<br/>

## Why the Engineering Background Matters

Most product managers hand off a spec and hope it comes back close to what they imagined. I can read the system underneath the feature — what a change costs in infrastructure, where a dependency will slow a sprint, what "technically simple" actually means for a given stack. That shortens the distance between a good idea and a shippable one, and it's why engineering teams trust my scoping.

<br/>

## How I Build Products

```
Understand → Discover → Define → Prioritize → Design → Build → Measure → Iterate
```

Discovery and prioritization set direction; the engineering background keeps that direction grounded in what's actually buildable, in what order, and at what cost.
